# BG3 Flow: a player's guide

This module makes our Foundry combats feel like Baldur's Gate 3. You do not need to learn anything new: most of it just happens. Here is what changes at the table, in plain words.

## 1. Exploring: the enemies find you (or not)

- Outside combat you walk freely. There is no "start combat" button for you to press.
- Enemies notice you when you walk into their range and they can see you: light and walls matter, their eyes matter (darkvision), and a hidden character is harder to see.
- Press the **eye** button on your token to hide. It rolls your Stealth (or lets you type a value). You stay hidden until combat starts, until you attack, or until you press it again. In the dark or in dim light, a good Stealth keeps you unseen; in bright light, hiding does nothing.
- The moment an enemy spots you, or someone draws blood, combat starts by itself. A dialog asks you to **roll your initiative**: press the button, your dice roll on screen. If you are away, the DM rolls for you.
- A chat line says who spotted whom.

## 2. Fighting: your group acts together

- Everyone on your side who is next to each other in the initiative order shares **one turn**, like a party in BG3. The chat says **Shared turn: Cleric, Rogue**. Move and act in any order between you.
- Everyone in the acting group shows the **turn marker** under their token, and a **blue circle** shows how far each of you can still walk this turn.
- Above your token, a **green dot** is your action and an **orange triangle** your bonus action, like in BG3. They go hollow when spent, and the game refuses a second action ("Rogue has no action left this turn"). Extra Attack still gives you your two swings; an off-hand attack takes the bonus action (a light weapon swung once your attacks are done counts as that off-hand swing, so pick *Offhand* in the dialog for the right damage); Action Surge gives you another action.
- You cannot move a token when it is not your group's turn: Foundry says "Not your turn".
- Your movement is **measured**. Walk your speed and no more; difficult terrain costs extra, like in the game. If you try to go further, the move is refused and the message tells you how much you used.
- **Dash** is an action on your sheet: use it and your movement doubles for the turn.
- When the last of you has finished, the DM (or the module) passes the turn.

## 3. Attacking: no dialogs, dice on screen

- Click a weapon or spell. An **orange circle** shows its range and a notice asks you to click the creature you want, every time, even if something was still marked from your last action (Esc cancels; with several targets, Enter confirms fewer). A spell you could cast with a higher slot asks which one, and if the higher level buys more targets (Hold Person) the circle comes back for the extra ones. Attacking from Hiding rolls with advantage. Then the attack rolls, the hit is checked, the damage rolls and applies. No pop-ups asking you how to roll. Everyone sees the dice.
- When you are done, press **End turn** (the tracker, or the chequered flag in your token's HUD). That ends only your part: the group's turn moves on once every player in it has ended, or when the DM moves it. Hiding costs your action (your bonus action if you have Cunning Action). If your character is dominated, the DM plays it and your clicks are refused; if it is confused, the DM's screen rolls the spell's d10 at your turn and the chat tells you what you must do.
- Saving throws roll by themselves too, in public.
- **Reactions** work like BG3: when someone hits you and you have Shield, or damages you and you have Uncanny Dodge, a prompt asks whether to use it, showing the attack roll. You have **30 seconds**; no answer means no reaction. Counterspell asks when an enemy casts.
- **Area spells** that let you spare your friends (Spirit Guardians and the like) ask you one question before the area appears: **Enemies only** or **Everyone**. Fireball does not ask; it hits everyone inside.
- Some spells keep hurting whoever stands or walks in their area each turn (Spirit Guardians, Moonbeam, Cloud of Daggers). That is automatic, but it runs on the caster's screen: keep your Foundry tab open, or the damage waits for you.

## 4. The monsters play themselves

- Ordinary monsters move and attack on their own: they pick targets, walk into range, shoot from a distance, and use their best attacks. The DM watches and can step in at any time.
- They are not dumb. A clever monster steps away from you before shooting, aims an area at the biggest cluster, and counts the hits it would take from your opportunity attacks before leaving your reach. A dumb one just charges.
- Badly hurt monsters may **flee**: they Dash away or run for an exit of the map. A monster that reaches the exit is out of the fight. A boss never flees.
- **Opportunity attacks** are yours: when a monster leaves your reach, a prompt asks whether you take the swing. Say yes and the attack rolls. With **Sentinel**, a hit stops the monster where it stands, and the module knows it.
- If your fighter uses the **Tunnel Fighter** stance, the module asks you itself when a monster moves inside your reach: **Attack** or **Pass**, 30 seconds.
- Big legendary monsters (dragons, liches) are played by the DM by hand. Their **lair** is not: at initiative count 20 the lair itself acts, choosing the lair action that hurts the party most, and aiming conditions at whoever is the biggest threat to it.

## 5. House rule: Enlarge

When you are enlarged, a Strength weapon attack adds your Strength modifier a second time instead of the usual 1d4. Dexterity attacks keep the 1d4. Nothing to do: the effect handles it.

## What you should do

- Keep your Foundry tab visible during combat: reactions, area damage and the initiative roll all happen on your screen.
- Answer prompts quickly; 30 seconds and they close.
- Move only on your group's turn, and watch the blue circle.
- If something looks wrong (a monster did something odd, a prompt never came), tell the DM: the module writes a note to them with the reason for every monster decision.
